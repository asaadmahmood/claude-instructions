# The First-Win Audit

**The entry offer for The Small Square's founder outreach. This is the thing you pitch when a founder accepts and asks "what do you do?" Don't freeze. Paste/say this.**

---

## The one-liner (say this on a call or in a thread)

It's the First-Win Audit. I take one core flow in your product, usually signup to first real value, and tear it down: a 30-45 minute annotated walkthrough plus a written doc showing exactly where you're losing users and how to fix each problem. You'll have it within 5 days, all I need is a login. It's $1,000, and you'll walk away with at least 5 specific, fixable problems costing you users, or it's free. I was the founding designer at Mattermost, so this is the exact work I've shipped for products engineers actually run on.

---

## The offer, broken down

**Name:** The First-Win Audit

**What they get:**
- A 30-45 min annotated Loom walkthrough of one core flow
- A written doc with annotated screenshots, specific friction points, and concrete fixes for each

**Scope:** ONE flow, not the whole product. Usually signup to first real value (activation). Flex to an adoption problem (a feature users can't understand / aren't adopting) if that's the pain they describe.

**Turnaround:** Within 5 days.

**Their effort:** Near zero. They hand over a login. That's it.

**Price:** $1,000, fixed.

**Guarantee:** At least 5 specific, fixable problems costing them users, or it's free. (You'll never pay out. Every early-stage product has 15+. The bar exists to signal confidence and reverse their risk, not because it's a real risk.)

**Proof / why they believe it'll work:** Founding designer at Mattermost (the team-collaboration / DevOps platform NASA and the US Air Force run on). This is shipped work, not theory.

---

## Two ways to aim it (diagnose, don't offer a menu)

Same offer, same price, same deliverable. You pick the framing based on the pain they describe. Lead with ONE. Never present both as options, a confused founder doesn't buy.

**Activation framing (default):** "I'll show you where you're losing users between signup and their first real win, and how to fix it." Use when they say signups don't stick, trial-to-paid is weak, people drop off early.

**Adoption framing:** "I'll show you why users find [feature] hard to understand and aren't adopting it, and how to fix that." Use when they say they built something nobody uses, or their best features go undiscovered.

---

## Why this offer is shaped the way it is (Hormozi value equation)

- **Dream outcome (up):** more signups reach value and stick = revenue. The teardown is the vehicle, not the product.
- **Perceived likelihood (up):** Mattermost founding-designer credential + the specificity guarantee. This is the strongest lever, lead with the credential.
- **Time delay (down):** 5 days. "You'll have it this week."
- **Effort/sacrifice (down):** "All I need is a login."

**Deliberately NOT doing** (would cheapen you with technical founders): bonus-stacking, countdown-timer urgency, fake scarcity. Your scarcity is real and quiet: you take only a couple of these a month because you run an agency. State it once, plainly, if at all.

---

## The natural next step (how the audit becomes the retainer)

The teardown covers one flow. Once delivered, the bigger paid work is the obvious next sentence: "The same problems exist across your dashboard / settings / onboarding, fixing those properly is the engagement." The audit is a paid trial of the embedded relationship you actually want, not a one-off.

---

## Pricing discipline

- $1,000 is a fast yes for a founder and already a bargain for your background. Don't discount it the first time someone hesitates, the price is a signal.
- The math only works if this is genuinely 3-5 days of focused effort. Keep scope to ONE flow. If it balloons into the whole app, you've sold a $10k engagement for $1k.
- Raise the price later once you have 2-3 done and can show samples.
